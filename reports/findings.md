## 1. Most of the value is in the first few calls
- The 1st call converts 5.6% of clients reached; by the 5th call it is
  2.4%, and by the 10th 1.1%.
- 96% of all subscriptions happen within 5 calls.
- **Capping at 5 calls** would save 15% of call attempts while keeping
  96% of subscriptions.
- Under the current assumptions the value-maximising cap is **6 calls**
  (the cap moves if cost per call or value per subscription changes - see the Excel workbook).

## 2. Targeting: score clients before calling
- Logistic regression trained on the first 75% of contacts (2008-05-01 to 2009-05-01), tested on the last 25%
  (2009-05-01 to 2010-11-01). Call duration and attempt count are excluded because neither is known before calling.
- Test AUC **0.703**. If the call centre only has capacity for 30% of the list, calling the
  highest-scored 30% captures **53%** of the test-window subscriptions (random calling: 30%).
- Adding month and macro-economic indicators *lowered* out-of-time AUC to
  0.636: interest rates in the test window are outside anything seen in training.
  They are useful for explaining the past (section 4), not for scoring the future.
- With unlimited capacity and the current assumptions, every decile is still profitable, so net value peaks at calling everyone (EUR 237,196); the model's value is in **prioritising limited calling capacity**.
- Caveat: the test window has a much higher base conversion (26%) than training,
  so the model is ranking well under a market shift, but absolute rates should not be carried forward.

## 3. Who and how
- Mobile beats landline: cellular 14.7% vs telephone 5.2%.
- Clients who said yes in a previous campaign convert at 65%
  (5.8x the average) - the clearest re-targeting list.
- Highest-lift segments (300+ clients each): retired 65+ (47%, 4.2x); student <25 (38%, 3.4x); student 25-34 (28%, 2.5x).

## 4. Market context
- Monthly conversion moves inversely with the 3-month Euribor rate (correlation -0.68 across 26 months):
  conversion rose as rates fell after 2008. This is a correlation across time, not proof of cause.

## 5. A reporting trap this project avoids
- Averaging the 26 monthly conversion rates gives **35.4%**.
  The correct pooled rate is **11.3%**. Small months (10-70 clients) with very high rates
  dominate a simple average. Every rate in the SQL layer and the DAX measures is a ratio of sums for this reason.
